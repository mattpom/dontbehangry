Fast Food Favorites Our Stomachs Never Forget

Every fast food menu has a graveyard, and some of the ghosts haunt harder than others.

This one goes out to the Bojangles' Chicken and Biscuit -- the simple, unglamorous combo of a hand-breaded fried chicken filet wedged into one of the chain's famously flaky, made-from-scratch biscuits. No fancy sauce, no brioche bun, no marketing gimmick. Just crunch, salt, and butter-laced dough doing exactly what a biscuit is supposed to do.

The Chicken and Biscuit

That simplicity was the point, and it was built into the chain from day one. Bojangles opened its first location in Charlotte, North Carolina, in 1977, built specifically around Cajun-seasoned fried chicken and scratch-made buttermilk biscuits rather than added on as an afterthought. Pairing that biscuit with the seasoned chicken felt less like a menu item and more like a regional inheritance passed down with the chain itself. For a lot of regulars, it wasn't fast food -- it was a drive-through version of Sunday breakfast.

Menus evolve, and chains constantly rotate items to make room for new limited-time offers or to simplify kitchen operations. When a longtime favorite like this quietly disappears from the board, it tends to leave a bigger void than something flashier would, precisely because it never needed to shout to earn its spot.

Gone for Good, or Just Gone for Now?

Not every disappearance is permanent, though. Arby's potato cakes are the textbook counter-example: crispy, fried patties of seasoned mashed potato that quietly held down a corner of the menu for decades before getting pulled from the national lineup. The backlash was loud enough -- petitions, social media campaigns, regulars mourning them like a death in the family -- that Arby's has brought them back more than once since, usually as limited-time or regional offers rather than a full permanent return. That on-again, off-again pattern turns a menu item into folklore, something fans half-believe they can summon back if they complain about it enough.

The Bojangles biscuit sandwich doesn't have that same revival track record, at least not yet. But the underlying appeal is identical: a small, dependable comfort that never needed a campaign behind it, only a habit built one drive-through visit at a time.

The Rest of the Graveyard

Bojangles and Arby's aren't alone in this. Taco Bell pulled its Mexican Pizza entirely in 2020 as part of a pandemic-era menu simplification, only to bring it back permanently in 2022 after a fan campaign turned it into one of the loudest "bring it back" stories in recent fast food memory. Burger King's Chicken Fries have followed a similar boom-and-bust rhythm for years, disappearing from menus for stretches and then returning as a limited-time event almost every time, treated less like a relaunch and more like a scheduled reunion. What connects all of these isn't the food itself so much as the pattern: a chain assumes an item is replaceable, discovers the fan base disagrees, and ends up managing the item's absence almost as carefully as it once managed its presence.

Why the Simple Stuff Sticks

It's a reminder that the fast food items people miss most usually aren't the ones with the biggest ad budgets. They're the reliable orders -- the one you didn't have to think about, the one your stomach filed away as a small, dependable comfort. That's what makes a "discontinued" tag sting more than it should for a five-dollar sandwich, and what keeps fans hitting refresh on rumors that a potato cake, a chicken fry, or a biscuit might make its way back for good.
