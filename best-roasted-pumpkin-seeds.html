How to Make the Best Roasted Pumpkin Seeds

Save the seeds. Make the crunch. A straightforward recipe for a salty, golden autumn snack.

Words by Don't Be Hangry  ·  Deeper Dive

Before you throw away the handful of seeds left inside a pumpkin, consider the snack sitting in front of you. A little oil, some seasoning and a hot oven can turn them into something worth putting in a bowl beside the television. The trick is giving them the right preparation before they reach the oven.

Start with the right seeds

This recipe is for whole pumpkin seeds with their pale shells still attached—the kind you scoop from a fresh pumpkin. The shells are edible. Packaged green pepitas have a different texture, so do not assume they need the same cooking time.

The recipe

For 1 cup cleaned whole pumpkin seeds

  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • ¼ teaspoon salt, with more to taste after roasting
  • Optional: ¼ teaspoon each garlic powder and smoked paprika
  • Oven: 350°F (about 177°C)

1. Rinse and dry thoroughly

Scoop out the seeds and separate them from the stringy pulp. Rinse in a colander, rubbing away any stubborn strands. Spread them on a clean kitchen towel and pat thoroughly dry. Excess surface water encourages steaming and delays browning; drying is the step to take seriously.

2. Season and spread

Toss the dried seeds with the oil, salt and optional spices. Spread them in one layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Use a second sheet if necessary rather than piling seeds together.

3. Roast and check

Roast at 350°F for approximately 12–15 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes. Larger seeds may need longer; other published recipes allow 15–20 minutes at this temperature. Treat the clock as a guide. Look for a golden color and toasted aroma, and let a sample cool before checking its crunch. Continue briefly if needed, checking often to avoid burning.

4. Cool before storing

Move the seeds off the hot baking sheet onto a plate or cool tray. Let them cool completely before sealing them in an airtight container. Store in a cool, dry place and enjoy within 1–2 weeks for best quality.

Do you need to boil them first?

Boiling is optional. Food educator Getty Stewart compares roasted seeds with and without that extra step and finds both worthwhile. For this simple version, rinse, dry and roast. There is no boiling step to fit into your evening.

Make the next batch your own

Keep a batch simply salted, or experiment with chili powder, cumin or a small pinch of cayenne. Taste before adding more salt, especially when using a prepared seasoning blend. Try the finished seeds on soup or a salad, or serve them straight from the bowl.

Save the seeds. Make the crunch. It is a satisfying way to get one more good thing from your pumpkin.
